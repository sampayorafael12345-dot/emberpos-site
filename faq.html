Getting started

How much does it cost?

₱599 per month per shop, after a free 30-day trial that doesn't ask for a card. That's everything: the register, kitchen screen, inventory, recipes, reports, payroll, the web back office. No per-device fees, no percentage of your sales. If it doesn't earn its keep in a month, walk away.

What do I need to run it?

Any ordinary Android phone or tablet (install from Google Play), or any computer or iPhone through the browser at emberpos.net/app. There is nothing special to buy. Most shops start with one secondhand tablet at the counter.

Can I try it without signing up?

Yes, that's what the live demo is for. It's the entire real app running on a pretend cafe with months of sales, staff, deliveries and stock counts already in it. Ring sales, break things, count stock, run payroll. It runs privately in your browser: nothing you do there touches anyone, and the Reset button gives you a fresh shop.

Do I have to encode my whole menu myself?

No. Send whatever you already have (a spreadsheet, a Word file, even a photo of your menu board) to atlasco.web@gmail.com and we encode your items, prices, ingredients and starting stock for you, free. You can also import a CSV yourself from Settings → Data, or load the sample menu to explore first.

Is there an iPhone app?

Not in the App Store yet. iPhone and iPad run the full app in Safari: open the app, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen, and you get an icon that opens full-screen like a normal app, register included.

Does it work without internet?

The Android app does, fully: selling, receipts, even signing in keep working through a brownout, and it syncs itself when the signal returns. In a browser the page needs to load once with a connection; after that it rides out short drops. For a shop with weak signal, the Android app is the safer choice.

Selling

Can one drink have sizes and add-ons with different prices?

Yes, and different recipes. A Large charges more and deducts a Large's milk; an extra shot charges ₱25 and takes 8 g of beans off the shelf. That's why the food-cost report can be trusted: the register and the stockroom are the same machine.

→ See it live: Ring up a sale in the demo.

How do discounts work? Senior / PWD?

Tap Discount on the cart: percent or amount, with a reason that goes on the record. You decide by role who is allowed to give one: a cashier can be limited or blocked, and every discount shows in the Activity Log and the sales reports with a name attached.

Can a customer pay half cash, half GCash?

Yes, split tender is built in. On the payment screen, tap two methods and give each its amount; GCash payments can carry a reference number so reconciling the wallet later is painless. There's also an e-wallet reconciliation tool under Money that compares your till records against your GCash statement.

Can I record utang the way we do in our notebook?

Yes. Attach the customer to the order and choose Utang at payment, and the amount goes on their tab instead of the drawer. The Money screen lists everyone who owes and for how long; record payments as they pay (or tap Full balance), set a per-person credit limit, and copy a ready-made reminder message to send on Messenger.

Someone paid, then changed their mind. Refund or void?

Void erases a mistake (double punch, wrong ticket), and the order stays on record as voided, with who and why. Refund gives money back on a real sale, whole or per line, and can restock the ingredients if the food never left. Both are permission-gated and both leave a paper trail; nothing is ever silently deleted.

What about holding an order for customers who order in waves?

Tap Hold and the ticket parks under Held Tickets; recall it when they're ready to add or pay. Dine-in tables work the same way: a table's ticket stays open until it's settled.

Kitchen & the board

How does the kitchen know what to cook?

Sending an order (or paying it) puts a ticket on the Kitchen screen instantly, so put a tablet by the pass and it rings on arrival. Every line shows size and add-ons exactly as typed. You can also pair a kitchen printer so a paper ticket prints at send.

→ See it live: The kitchen screen.

What is the Now Serving board?

A customer-facing screen of order numbers: preparing on one side, ready to claim on the other. It feeds itself from the till and the kitchen; nobody types anything. Any spare screen or tablet facing the queue will do.

Can customers order by scanning a QR code?

Yes, QR self-ordering is built in. Print the code on the table; customers browse the menu on their own phone and their order lands on your kitchen screen. You approve it before it cooks, so walk-away orders never cost you food.

Stock & recipes

Does selling really deduct my ingredients automatically?

Yes, that's the heart of the app. Every menu item carries a recipe; paying an order deducts every line of it, including the size's own recipe, the add-ons, and takeout packaging when the order is to-go. No end-of-day encoding, no clipboard.

How do deliveries work? My supplier short-delivers sometimes.

Receiving opens a count sheet first: your crew types what actually came off the truck, line by line, against the delivery receipt. Stock, costs and the bill book what you counted, and the receiving report says both numbers wherever they disagree ("received 2 of 3 ordered"). COD paid from the drawer shows up in the shift count automatically, and the report prints for the delivery man to sign.

→ See it live: Receive a delivery.

What happens during a stock count: do we stop selling?

Your choice: you can pause selling while the crew counts so the shelf holds still, or let the sheet wait and stamp expected quantities at apply time. Counts can be typed in kitchen units (kilos and grams, liters and milliliters, packs). Ember refuses an impossible count: 644 kg of matcha gets questioned at the door, not discovered in the report. And a count applied after midnight books to the trading day that just ended, so shrinkage never leaks into tomorrow.

We make things in-house: sauces, cold brew, sisig mix. Can it cost those?

Yes. An in-house item has its own recipe: record a batch and the raw ingredients deduct, the finished stock adds, and the batch's true cost flows into every product that uses it. The food-cost report even names which batch drew the milk.

→ See it live: The prep station.

How do I know what to buy before we run out?

The Shopping List watches how fast each ingredient actually moves and answers "what do I need for the next N days?" with quantities, in packs, grouped by supplier. Tick what you want and it becomes draft purchase orders. Low-stock items also flag themselves on the dashboard and the sidebar the moment they cross the threshold you set.

Where does wasted food go?

Waste & Spoilage, recorded in the moment, either an ingredient (the milk turned) or a whole product (a wrong latte), which deducts exactly what its recipe would have used. The report totals it in pesos, the dashboard nags you with the week's number, and a printable slip can be signed by whoever witnessed it.

Money & reports

Can it tell me if I'm actually making money?

That's the Profit & Loss: revenue from the till, ingredient cost from recipes and the stock ledger, labor from the time clock, expenses from what you record, all the way down to net profit, with nothing hand-typed in between. Beside it: cash flow (money in and out per day), break-even, and food-cost per product so you know which items earn and which leak.

→ See it live: Profit & Loss, where the demo cafe runs a 29% net margin.

What are Receive / To vault / Deposited / Picked up on the cash screen?

The cash custody chain: where the money physically is after a drawer closes, so cash is never unaccounted for between the count and the bank. Receive: the manager confirms it's in their hands (press it the moment you take the cash). To vault: locked in the shop's safe. Deposited: it reached the bank, with a deposit-slip number. Picked up: the owner or an authorized person collected it, by name. Every step stamps the amount, the time and the person; the owner can verify each one later.

My accountant wants the numbers. What can I export?

Everything, as CSV: sales books, orders, expenses, payroll, inventory, recipes with costs. Export Reports gathers them in one place. Your data is yours: there's no lock-in, and you can take a full backup any time from Settings → Data.

Is EmberPOS BIR-accredited?

EmberPOS is not a BIR-accredited cash register machine / POS provider. Receipts print your registered name, address and TIN in a standard layout, and every document carries the "not valid for claiming input taxes" line where the law requires it. Many small shops run Ember for operations alongside their BIR-registered receipt booklet. If you need formal accreditation, talk to your accountant about your shop's specific registration first.

Staff

Can my cashier see my profits?

Only if you let them. Access rights are per role: a cashier can be limited to the till and kitchen; reports, settings, voids, discounts, payroll are each their own switch. Everyone signs in as themselves, so every action in the shop has a name on it, and the Activity Log keeps the full trail.

→ See it live: Staff & access rights.

How do time-in / time-out and payroll work?

Staff punch the time clock (even from the login screen, without opening the register). Payroll computes a period from those punches: regular hours, overtime past 8 hours at 1.25×, each person's wage, less SSS / PhilHealth / Pag-IBIG amounts you set per employee, and saves the run as a permanent record, payslip-ready.

Someone forgot to clock out. Now what?

Fix it in the web back office: emberpos.net/office → Staff → Hours worked. A shift that's still open says so. Press Correct, set the real finish time, save. Payroll uses the corrected hours. (In the app itself you can adjust break minutes; the full correction lives in the office.)

Two cashiers share one till. Whose drawer is short?

Never both, and that's the point of shifts. Each drawer belongs to whoever opened it; a break hands the drawer over with a count, and the cover's cash is their own. A shortage belongs to exactly one person, on record, which protects the honest ones.

Bigger shops

I have two branches. Can I see both?

Yes, branches are built in. Each branch is its own shop (₱599/month each) with its own staff and stock; the owner switches between them in the app, and the web Back Office puts every branch on one page: combined sales, transfers between branches, and the same reports across all of them.

Can I watch the shop from home?

Open the app or the Back Office from any browser and everything is live: current drawer, who's clocked in, today's sales, the kitchen queue. Owners tell us this is the feature that finally let them take a day off.

Practical bits

Which printers work?

Ordinary ESC/POS thermal receipt printers, the ₱1,500-3,000 kind sold everywhere, over LAN/Wi-Fi, with a cash-drawer kick if the drawer is wired to the printer. Receipt printer and kitchen printer pair separately in Settings → Printers. One note: these printers only speak plain characters, so receipt text should stick to ordinary letters and numbers.

Is my data safe? What if my tablet dies?

Cloud shops sync continuously: sign in on any other device and the shop is there, whole. Data is isolated per shop and backed up for you. On top of that you can export a full backup any time, and account deletion (with everything in it) is yours on request: how that works.

The app says an update is available. Do I have to?

Updates come through Google Play like any app and never touch your data. We nudge only when a version genuinely matters, usually because a shop asked for something and it shipped. Most updates come from exactly that: a message from a shop like yours.

I found a bug / I want a feature. Who do I talk to?

The Write to us box inside the app (Help & Support) goes straight to the developer's phone. Most messages get a reply the same day, and shop requests routinely ship within the week. Or email atlasco.web@gmail.com.
